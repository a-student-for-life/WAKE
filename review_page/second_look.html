How to decide
  1. Name. The AIS name should match the report. Spacing, punctuation and prefixes (M/V, F/V, TUG, THE) don't matter. A "loose" match means the report's name sits inside a longer AIS name; accept it only if the extra part looks like a suffix (a number, "II") and nothing else disagrees.
  2. Type. The AIS type should fit the report. Small US work boats often broadcast no type; that is neutral. A clear clash (a fishing boat in the report, a cargo ship or pleasure craft in AIS) counts against.
  3. Size. Lengths within about 15% support; over 30% apart counts against. A missing length is neutral.
  4. Place and time. With an exact reported position, within about 5 km (3 km on a river) supports and beyond 25 km counts against, unless the track heads for the spot and AIS ends first. With only a town, within about 15 km supports and beyond 40 km counts against, unless the report puts the incident offshore of that town.
  5. Behaviour. A grounding or sinking should stop or end near the spot; a collision should show a stop or sudden change; a reported discharge underway should show the vessel moving there.

Missing AIS near the report time is common on rivers, offshore and for small boats, and AIS often stops when a vessel sinks; a gap is not a no by itself. Report times are assumed to be local time and can be off by an hour or be filed late. When the facts point both ways, or are too thin, choose Unsure.
